### Credit Scoring Business Understanding

Basel II requires that credit risk decisions be traceable and justifiable, not just accurate. If a customer is denied credit, the bank needs to be able to explain why. This shapes several choices in this project: every experiment is logged to MLflow (parameters, metrics, artifacts) so the process is reproducible and auditable, and the plan is to pair the final model with SHAP and LIME so that individual predictions can be explained in terms of the actual features driving them, rather than treated as a black box.

### Proxy Variable

The dataset contains transaction records but no historical loan outcomes, i.e. there's no column saying who actually defaulted. To train a supervised model at all, a proxy target (is_high_risk) is constructed instead, based on RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary) behavior: customers are clustered into three groups by transaction engagement, and the cluster representing the least active, lowest-spending, longest-inactive segment is labeled high-risk. The risk is that this proxy is an assumption about correlation, not a measurement of actual default. A customer might be inactive for reasons unrelated to creditworthiness (they could have switched platforms, or bought seasonally) and get penalized as "high-risk" without ever being a genuine credit risk. Conversely, an actively transacting customer could still default. Because the label was constructed rather than observed, predictions from this model should be treated as a risk-informed heuristic rather than a validated default probability, until it can be checked against real repayment outcomes.

### WLogistic Regression with WoE (Interpretable Model) vs High Performance models (e.g. Gradient Boosting) in a regulated financial context

A simple model like Logistic Regression produces coefficients that map directly to risk reasoning, therefore they are easy to explain to a rejected applicant or a regulator, but it may not capture more complex, non-linear patterns in customer behavior, which can limit predictive accuracy. A high-performance model like Gradient Boosting can typically capture those patterns better and produce stronger metrics, but its internal decision logic isn't directly readable from the model itself. That's the reasoning behind pairing the stronger model with SHAP and LIME in this project: aiming to get the accuracy benefit of a more complex model without losing the ability to explain individual decisions, which Basel II's documentation expectations require.
